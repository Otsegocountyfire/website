Resident Guide

Know Your Protection

MI Prevention

Michigan’s Bureau of Fire Services partners with local fire departments to provide fire safety education, and has supplied state-funded smoke alarms for installation in eligible owner-occupied homes.

Check local availability. New state alarm distribution is paused for fiscal year 2027 because no funds were appropriated. Alarms already supplied to OCFA may be available while local supplies last. A request does not guarantee equipment or an appointment.

Alarms from the program are installed during a home visit; they are not handed out. Renters may ask for guidance about their household’s needs.

MI Prevention Information ↗

Install, Test, and Plan

  • Cover every sleeping area. Install smoke alarms inside each bedroom, outside each separate sleeping area, and on every level, including the basement.
  • Test monthly. Follow the manufacturer’s instructions and replace smoke alarms 10 years after they were made.
  • Practice your escape. Know two ways out of every room, pick an outdoor meeting place, and practice with everyone at home.
Installation Guidance ↗

Carbon Monoxide

  • Install CO alarms outside each sleeping area and on every level of your home.
  • If a CO alarm sounds, get everyone outside to fresh air right away, then call 9-1-1.
  • Know the signs. Headache, dizziness, weakness, upset stomach, vomiting, chest pain, and confusion can all be CO poisoning.
  • Run generators outdoors only, more than 20 feet from windows, doors, and vents. Never use one in a home or garage, even with the doors open.
  • Keep heat safe. Have your furnace, water heater, wood stove, and chimney checked every year, keep outside vents clear of snow, and never heat your home with an oven or grill.
